Whether you own a single-family home, a small apartment building, or a commercial space in town, we have the know-how and equipment to handle the full range of heating and cooling work. Most homeowners we work with need one of three things: a repair, a replacement, or regular maintenance. Repairs are usually straightforward: a failed capacitor, a dirty flame sensor, a slow refrigerant leak, or a seized blower motor. Replacements get a proper load calculation, not a guess based on the old unit's tonnage, which keeps temperatures even and stops the new equipment from short-cycling its way to an early failure. Maintenance visits include coil cleaning, a refrigerant charge check, safety control testing, and a static pressure reading, which does more than anything else to extend the life of your system.

When Your System Fails in Houston

If your system quits, how quickly help arrives matters more than anything else. For that reason, we keep scheduling flexible across the area and put a real person on the phone. Emergency calls usually get a technician within a day, and we prioritize a failed furnace in a cold snap or a dead AC in the heat as top priority.

Common emergencies we handle include air conditioners blowing warm air, outdoor units that hum but won't start, furnaces that ignite and then shut down, breakers that trip every time the system runs, frozen evaporator coils, clogged condensate drains backing up into the furnace cabinet, and thermostats that have simply given up. Our trucks carry the capacitors, contactors, igniters, flame sensors, universal control boards, and fittings needed to complete most repairs in a single visit. We provide upfront pricing every time, so you know exactly what the work will cost before we begin.

A few minutes of caution before we arrive can head off a much larger repair. An iced-over AC should be turned off at the thermostat with the fan set to "on", which lets the coil thaw faster. Running it frozen can flood the drain pan and damage the compressor. A short-cycling furnace, or any burning smell, means it's time to kill power at the furnace switch and call us right away.

How Houston Homeowners Can Extend HVAC Life

Most mid-season breakdowns start with maintenance that never happened, and the habits that prevent them are simple. Swap your filter every one to three months, clear vegetation at least two feet from the outdoor condenser, and rinse the condenser coil every spring. Then book a professional tune-up twice a year, one for cooling and one for heating, so our technicians can catch small problems while they're still cheap to fix.

Equipment age matters just as much. With regular service, central air typically lasts 12 to 15 years, a furnace 15 to 20, and a heat pump 10 to 15. Once a system passes that range and starts showing warning signs, like higher energy bills, rooms that never reach the setpoint, or repeat service calls, it's smart to start planning a replacement. A planned replacement costs less and is far less stressful than scrambling for a new system during the worst heat wave of summer.

If comfort problems do show up, acting quickly keeps them small. A weak capacitor is a quick fix. The same capacitor ignored for a season can take the compressor with it and turn a minor repair into a full system replacement. It's smart to keep our number saved on your phone so you can reach us at the first sign of trouble. Early intervention saves time, money, and sleepless nights.
