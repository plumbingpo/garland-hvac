Many Garland, TX homeowners replacing an aging furnace and AC are choosing a single heat pump instead, and the reason is efficiency. A heat pump does not burn fuel or heat a resistance coil; it moves heat. In cooling mode it carries heat out of the house, and in heating mode a reversing valve sends the refrigerant the other way so the compressor gathers heat from outdoor air and releases it indoors. That lets it deliver two to three times more heat than the electricity it consumes. Modern cold-climate equipment with inverter-driven compressors keeps working well below freezing, which the older units behind the technology's old reputation simply could not do.

Getting that performance takes design work up front, and this is where Garland HVAC earns the job. Sizing a heat pump means reconciling two loads: the summer cooling demand and the winter heating demand, and finding the balance point where outdoor temperature meets the unit's capacity curve. Our experienced technicians run the load calculation, weigh single-stage, two-stage, and variable-speed inverter options against your climate and ducts, and design the backup heat correctly, whether that is electric strips staged to assist below the balance point or a dual-fuel pairing with a gas furnace that takes over in the deepest cold. We also verify the electrical service and the duct system, because heat pumps deliver their heat at lower supply temperatures than furnaces and need real airflow to feel right.

The installation work is where careful habits pay off. We pressure-test and evacuate the line set to a verified vacuum, weigh in the charge and confirm it with superheat and subcooling, and raise the outdoor unit on a stand when your winters call for defrost drainage. The condensate gets proper handling, and the thermostat is set so staging, backup heat lockouts, and defrost behave exactly as the engineering intends. Commissioning covers both modes, with every reading recorded. If you want a heat pump installed by people who measure, call (972) 920-8553 for an assessment and a firm quote.

Why Professional Heat Pump Installation Matters

No residential system is less forgiving of shortcuts than a heat pump, because every error shows up in both seasons. A slightly wrong charge drags down summer efficiency and winter capacity alike. Without a calculated balance point, backup heat strips can quietly carry the load at several times the cost, and the promised savings never appear. Default thermostat settings may run backup heat unnecessarily or fight the defrost cycle. And in a Garland winter, an outdoor unit resting directly on the ground can end up frozen into its own defrost runoff with the coil locked in ice.

Our experienced technicians design and install against all of it: loads calculated for both seasons, equipment chosen for the actual capacity curve at your winter design temperature, backup heat staged and locked out correctly, the unit elevated for defrost drainage, and the charge weighed and verified rather than guessed. Commissioning runs in both modes, including confirming the reversing valve and watching a defrost cycle complete. Refrigerant work is federally regulated, and inverter-driven equipment adds electronics that reward a careful setup. How to Prevent Heat Pump Installation Problems

A little routine maintenance goes a long way toward avoiding a mid-season breakdown. Follow these tips from our experienced technicians:

  • Clean the outdoor coil each spring for a strong start to summer
  • Check your first winter bill, since an unexpected jump often points to overworked backup heat
  • Book twice-yearly maintenance so charge and defrost stay verified in both modes
  • Let the system run steady; big setbacks trigger expensive backup heat on recovery
  • Replace filters on time, since restricted airflow hurts heat pump performance year-round
  • Light frost and an occasional defrost hiss are normal in winter, but solid ice calls for service
  • Keep the outdoor unit clear of snow drifts, leaves, and shrubs year-round
